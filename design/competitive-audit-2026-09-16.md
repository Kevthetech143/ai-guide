# Competitive audit — ai-guide vs the 6 most successful AI directories

Date: 2026-09-16. Research only; nothing on the site, data, or build was touched.
Our site: https://kevthetech143.github.io/ai-guide/ — 60 providers, 36 plain-English
question pages, 8 categories, start-here picker, 135 pages, no ads, no affiliate
links, 8th-grade reading level, per-card "last checked" dates.

## 1. The 6 competitors (all live today, verified by visit)

### 1.1 There's An AI For That — https://theresanaiforthat.com
- **What it is:** the original "front page of AI" feed. Homepage visited 2026-09-16:
  a chronological launch feed (cards timestamped 5m, 10m, 40m, 1h, 2h…).
- **Count listed:** not stated on the homepage (unverified).
- **Money:** inline ads labeled "Ad" with view counts ("Ad 130", "Ad 57k", "Ad 300k",
  "Ad 40k" seen in the feed) + a paid membership/premium plan exists
  (unverified — premium page not opened).
- **Audience:** early adopters, AI hobbyists, founders watching new launches.
- **First-visit answer path:** search box + category filters + the firehose feed;
  there is no guided "tell me your problem" flow.
- **Reading level:** technical. Card copy is vendor-written and jargon-dense
  ("Agentic knowledge work, (Elo-500)/2000", "MCP Supported inputs", "Endpoint
  Accuracy Index").
- **Price currency:** every card shows "Pricing model: Freemium" and "Paid options
  from $28.99/month / Billing frequency Monthly" — but no last-checked or
  published date seen on cards. Dated: no.
- **Honest about downsides:** no. Descriptions are launch announcements
  ("Introducing MNKI Video: An end-to-end video generation and editing platform
  built for modern workflows"). Comments exist but none read by me (unverified).

### 1.2 Futurepedia — https://www.futurepedia.io
- **What it is:** business-facing AI directory + education platform. Homepage
  visited 2026-09-16.
- **Count listed:** homepage says "We've categorized 2503 AI tools into 10
  categories." The same page also claims "4,000+ curated tools" in its
  about-copy. Those two numbers conflict; treat both as the site's own claims,
  verified as *present* but inconsistent with each other.
- **Money:** paid education. "Skill Leap AI" courses, "Claim your free access …
  Upgrade anytime to 29+ courses" — freemium-to-paid learning. Sponsored
  listings/advertise options exist per third-party descriptions (unverified).
  (A LinkedIn page for the company, crawled recently, says Futurepedia is now
  part of HubSpot Media — unverified third-party claim, not on futurepedia.io.)
- **Audience:** professionals, teams, executives ("where businesses figure out AI").
- **First-visit answer path:** search bar ("I want help with my math homework"
  style queries per their FAQ), category browse, daily newsletter.
- **Reading level:** business-professional, not plain-English. Jargon appears
  without gloss.
- **Price currency:** cards show pricing model; daily updates claimed
  ("updated daily" per their FAQ). Card-level dates not seen by me. Dated: partially.
- **Honest about downsides:** no. Listings are vendor descriptions; reviews exist
  but no structured downsides section seen on the homepage. The one real
  honesty-adjacent asset is the YouTube review channel (unverified content).

### 1.3 Toolify — https://www.toolify.ai
- **What it is:** a giant tool grid, SEO-scale directory. Homepage visited
  2026-09-16.
- **Count listed:** not stated on the homepage (unverified; historically claimed
  tens of thousands — unverified).
- **Money:** a "Featured*" sponsored section tops the homepage — paid placement is
  the visible revenue. (Affiliate/ad details unverified.)
- **Audience:** everyone and no one — a volume SEO play aimed at search traffic,
  plus developers (many listings are API gateways).
- **First-visit answer path:** an endless card grid; search and filters exist.
  No guidance, no picker, no editorial curation visible.
- **Reading level:** mixed. Many cards are vendor marketing copy, some of it
  dubious: observed live listings included "Get ChatGPT Plus for only $12.95
  with NO credit card required" (reseller-style) and "The only AI API marketplace
  that fingerprint-verifies every provider … up to 90% off". No verification or
  fact-check layer visible.
- **Price currency:** "Free" badges on cards; no last-checked or published dates
  seen. Dated: no.
- **Honest about downsides:** no. Nothing critical about any tool is visible on
  the homepage.

### 1.4 Product Hunt, AI topic — https://www.producthunt.com/topics/artificial-intelligence
- **What it is:** a launch feed for new AI products, ranked by community upvotes.
  Page visited 2026-09-16.
- **Count listed:** thousands of AI launches (unverified — exact count not visible;
  the topic page is a feed, not a catalog).
- **Money:** sells featured/paid launch placements (unverified — advertising page
  not opened).
- **Audience:** early adopters, makers, investors.
- **First-visit answer path:** chronological feed sorted by launches of the day;
  filters by topic. A first-timer sees launches, not answers.
- **Reading level:** startup jargon ("agentic refactoring", "multimodal APIs").
- **Price currency:** launch posts include pricing sometimes; no currency
  guarantee. Dated: launch-day only.
- **Honest about downsides:** partially — community comments and reviews can
  criticize (not verified by me reading any), but the page design celebrates
  launches.

### 1.5 G2, AI software categories — https://www.g2.com/categories/ai (blocked to
my fetcher today; evidence from G2's own report pages and review-preview page)
- **What it is:** B2B software review marketplace with Grid® quadrant reports.
  Evidence: G2's AI Agents and AI Customer Support Agents Grid report pages and
  a G2 "Research Board Preview" page (all g2.com), visited via search
  2026-09-16.
- **Count listed:** per-category, e.g. dozens of vendors per AI subcategory
  (unverified aggregate).
- **Money:** vendors pay for profiles, boosted visibility, and report licensing.
  Ratings themselves are not for sale: G2's own report text states "G2 does not
  allow for paid placement in any of its ratings" (cdn2.hubspot.net copy of a G2
  Grid report).
- **Audience:** B2B technology buyers with IT departments and procurement processes.
- **First-visit answer path:** category pages, comparison grids, review filters.
  Assumes the visitor already knows what software category they need.
- **Reading level:** enterprise. "Market Presence", "AI SDRs", "Grid® Scoring
  Methodology v4.0/v7.0".
- **Price currency:** review and report quarters are dated ("Summer 2026 …
  based on reviews collected through April 28, 2026"). Pricing fields exist per
  product. Dated: yes, quarterly.
- **Honest about downsides:** yes — the only competitor that is structurally
  honest. Their product comparison pages carry "Key Strengths" AND "Potential
  Drawbacks" sections drawn from user reviews (e.g. ChatGPT 4.6/5 from 2,704
  reviews: users report "inaccurate", "limitations in accuracy and source
  reliability"; Gemini: "usage limitations", "inaccurate responses"; TESS AI:
  "credit system issues … high costs and lack of transparency"). Observed on
  https://www.g2.com/assistant/preview?product_slugs%5B%5D=chatgpt… (URL as
  returned by search).

### 1.6 Artificial Analysis — https://artificialanalysis.ai
- **What it is:** independent LLM/model benchmark lab. Homepage visited 2026-09-16.
- **Count listed:** hundreds of model evaluations across leaderboards
  (unverified exact count).
- **Money:** sells API/data access and enterprise analysis (unverified — pricing
  page not opened; homepage shows no ads).
- **Audience:** engineers, AI teams, enterprises choosing models.
- **First-visit answer path:** leaderboards (Intelligence Index, cost-per-task
  Pareto charts, speed/latency tables). A visitor picks a chart, not an answer.
- **Reading level:** expert. "Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3",
  "Elo", "Pareto line", "TTFT", "AA-Omniscience Non-Hallucination Rate".
- **Price currency:** the strongest in the field. Changelog entries are dated to
  the day ("New language model evaluation · 15 Sept", "13 Sept", "10 Sept",
  "7 Sept"), per-model USD-per-1M-token pricing tables shown. Dated: yes, daily.
- **Honest about downsides:** yes, objectively — a benchmark IS a downsides
  engine. Low scores are published as data (e.g. hallucination-rate metrics,
  cost-vs-intelligence Pareto). No editorial prose, but the numbers don't flatter.

**Overall average: 3.0 / 5. Strongest: (b) jargon-free readability, 5.0.
Weakest: (e) breadth of coverage, 1.3.**

Why these scores (one line each, grounded in what I saw):

- (a) 3–4: our start-here picker answers a stated need in ≤3 taps; the feeds
  (TAAFT, Toolify, Product Hunt) answer nothing — they scroll. G2 and AA don't
  try to answer at all, which is why we outscore them on (a) even while losing
  everywhere else.
- (b) 5 across the board: our 8th-grade plain sentences vs TAAFT's "MCP
  Supported inputs", Futurepedia's business prose, Toolify's reseller marketing
  copy, Product Hunt's startup jargon, G2's "Market Presence", AA's "Elo/TTFT".
- (c) 2–4: we put last-checked dates on cards (better than Toolify's dateless
  "Free" badges and Product Hunt's launch-day pricing), but G2's quarterly
  report quarters and AA's daily-dated changelog beat our weekly manual review.
- (d) 3–4: we're honest by design; everyone else except G2 ("Potential
  Drawbacks" from real users) and AA (benchmarks as downsides) publishes
  vendor marketing. We match them on intent but not on evidence volume.
- (e) 1–2: 60 providers vs thousands. This is the single biggest gap and it's
  not close. Futurepedia's own homepage claims 2,503 tools in 10 categories —
  40x our coverage.
- (f) 2–3: we have no named reviewers, no user reviews, no published methodology,
  no traffic, no press. G2 has verified reviews and a stated no-paid-ratings
  policy; AA has a named methodology and changelog; Futurepedia has ratings and
  a newsletter audience. Our only trust asset is "no ads, no affiliates" —
  which nobody knows about because nobody knows us.
- (g) 3–4: our static HTML is fast and uncluttered; TAAFT/Toolify are ad-heavy
  grids; G2 and AA are heavy dashboards. We win on lightness, not features.
- (h) 2: all six competitors dominate "best AI tool" search results. We are a
  new GitHub Pages site with 135 pages, no backlinks, no brand. We are
  effectively invisible.

## 3. Where we LOSE — brutally, competitor by competitor

- **Breadth.** Everyone has 10–1000x our catalog. A visitor asking about a tool
  we don't cover gets nothing; on Toolify/TAAFT/Futurepedia they get something.
  60 providers is a demo, not a directory.
- **Trust signals.** G2 publishes verified reviews and a no-paid-ratings policy;
  AA publishes its methodology and a dated changelog; Futurepedia shows ratings
  and a real audience; Product Hunt shows named makers and upvotes. We show:
  a "last checked" date. There is no named author, no editorial policy page, no
  "we take no money from anyone" statement anyone can find, no reviews, no
  user feedback loop. For a non-technical audience — the people most vulnerable
  to bad advice — our trust page is the weakest asset in the comparison.
- **Findability.** We lose this dimension to all six. They have years of
  backlinks, brand searches, and press; we have a fresh github.io subdomain.
  Our 36 question pages are the right instinct, but 36 pages against
  directories with thousands of indexed pages is not a strategy, it's a start.
- **Freshness at scale.** Futurepedia claims daily updates; TAAFT's feed is
  real-time (timestamps of minutes); AA dates every evaluation to the day.
  Our weekly manual review of 60 cards is honest but cannot scale, and nothing
  on the site proves the cadence is being kept except the dates themselves.
- **Honesty with evidence.** We write honest downsides; G2 and AA back theirs
  with thousands of user data points and measured benchmarks. Our honesty is
  one editorial voice with no citations. A skeptical visitor can verify G2's
  claims; ours they must take on faith.
- **The honest summary:** our only defensible advantages are readability (b=5.0)
  and a guided answer path for non-technical people (a≈3.3). Everything else —
  breadth, trust, findability, freshness, evidence — we lose. The readability
  advantage is real but fragile: it only matters if anyone arrives (h=2.0).

## 4. The 5 highest-leverage things to build next (ranked by visitor value / effort)

1. **Publish a "How we review / we take no money" trust page, and name a human
   reviewer.** One plain page: who checks the cards, the weekly cadence, the
   no-ads/no-affiliate/no-paid-placement policy, how to report a wrong price.
   Effort: an afternoon. Competitor doing it: G2 (methodology + "no paid
   placement in ratings" policy) and Artificial Analysis (named methodology,
   dated changelog).
2. **Make freshness self-evident: a site-wide "last full review" stamp plus an
   automated stale-card flag.** Show one date on the homepage ("every card
   re-checked within the last 7 days" — true only if the weekly task keeps it
   true) and auto-flag any card older than the cadence. Effort: small build.py
   change. Competitor doing it: Artificial Analysis (every evaluation dated to
   the day; staleness is impossible to miss).
3. **Expand the question pages from 36 to ~200 long-tail questions people
   actually type.** This is the only realistic findability lever we control:
   one excellent answer page per real query ("best free AI for homework",
   "which AI can I run on my own computer"). Effort: steady editorial work, no
   code. Competitor doing it: none directly — the directories all chase
   head-term SEO; the question-shaped hole is ours to take.
4. **Turn "honest downsides" from prose into a structured "watch out for" block
   with a source.** Every card gets: what it's bad at, who it's NOT for, and one
   link backing the claim. Effort: editorial pass over the 60 providers. 
   Competitor doing it: G2 ("Potential Drawbacks" from user reviews) — theirs
   is user-powered, ours can be editor-powered, which is faster to ship.
5. **Add a feedback loop: "Was this helpful? / report a wrong price" on every
   card, with the corrections published.** Gives visitors a reason to return,
   generates the user-trust signals we lack, and feeds the weekly review with
   real reports. Effort: medium (form + inbox + publishing step). Competitor
   doing it: Product Hunt (community comments/corrections) and G2 (user reviews).

  "2M+ YouTube subscribers" from LinkedIn, "now part of HubSpot Media") are
  third-party claims I could not verify on the competitors' own pages — all
  marked unverified above and excluded from scoring.
- Product Hunt's AI topic page rendered nearly blank to my text fetcher
  (JS-heavy); my Product Hunt claims rest on the SEO blurb visible on that page
  plus the known feed structure, which I did not fully render. Scored
  conservatively.
- G2's category page would not load for me; G2 claims rest on G2's own Grid
  report pages and their review-preview page (all g2.com), which loaded.
- No competitor states a tool count on its homepage except Futurepedia (2,503
  in 10 categories, plus a conflicting "4,000+ curated tools" on the same page).
- Nothing on any competitor page I opened showed card-level "last checked"
  dates except Artificial Analysis's changelog and G2's quarterly report
  windows — both of which beat our per-card weekly dates on freshness.
